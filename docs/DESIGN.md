# Design System: Flight Plan

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Line Diagram"**

Flight Plan reads like transit signage. Every repository is a line with its own colour and a lettered roundel, and every review target is a stop on that line. The status of a review artifact is drawn in the stop's ring, so it reads before any word does. An ink sign band runs across the top of every page and carries the amber logo and the product name; a page puts its own header in the band, as the review page does.

The panels below the band are warm timetable paper in light mode and warm charcoal in dark mode, chosen by `prefers-color-scheme`. Structure comes from ruled lines and one shared column registration, not from boxes. Depth is tonal: the paper, the code surface, the fields and the quiet fill sit at clearly different lightness, so each reads as its own material without a shadow. The density is that of a timetable: compact rows, generous space between lines, nothing floating. The system replaces the sidebar, table and badge dashboard with the diagram itself.

The frontend only renders. Labels, counts, line colours, roundel letters, monograms, the status filter, the filtering itself, highlighted code tokens, diagram positions and the order of everything come from the backend; components pick a variant from a discriminant prop and draw it.

The review page keeps the layout of the legacy review panel: one full-width page read top to bottom, with the review header in the sign band, a section rail in the left gutter, and each segment's Context Section, Journey Flow Diagrams, Blast Diagram and Code Walkthrough in order at its right. The line appears there only as a gutter marker. The rail draws it to show where the reader is, and the content beside it is set in ruled bands, framed code panels and diagram boards, not as stops on a line.

**Key Characteristics:**
- A 4px vertical line per repository, in that repository's colour, with stops as rings on it.
- Seven ring states, one per branch status, each a different shape.
- An ink sign band, 56px tall, sticky at the top of every page, on the page column or at full width. On a review it carries a 4px rule in the repository's line colour along its lower edge.
- A 2px ink rule at the head of every line, hairline rules between sections; no cards, no shadows. Only code panels and diagram boards are framed.
- Marks that differ by shape before colour: stop rings, finding marks, blast file marks, edge strokes and arrowheads.
- One grotesk at two weights, with its fixed-width companion for code and code-like text.
- Amber is the primary colour: it fills every primary button, with ink text, and marks what waits on the reviewer, as a ring and a bold label.
- Text in the line colour where it names or locates the line: a repository's name, and on a review its headings and the labels of the section rail.
- State changes in place at 180ms ease-out; nothing reflows.

## Colors

Warm paper and a warm near-black ink carry six saturated line colours and one rationed amber. Every neutral is tinted toward the paper, at hue 60 to 90; none is a pure grey or a blue slate. Code sits on its own surface, with syntax colours that take their hues from the line palette. All values are OKLCH custom properties in `packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css`; each token has a light value and a `-dark` sibling in the frontmatter, and the stylesheet switches between them by `prefers-color-scheme`.

### Primary
- **Sign Ink** (`sign`, `foreground`): the sign band and body text share one ink in light mode. In dark mode the band drops to a deeper ink than the panel. A control that is switched on inverts to this ink.
- **Primary Amber** (`primary`): Flight Plan's primary colour. It fills every primary button, with ink text (`primary-foreground`) in both themes, as on airport wayfinding, and tints ticked checkboxes and radios through `accent-color`. It is the logo's amber at button strength, brighter than `signal`. The same ink draws the 4px rule between segments, the 2px border of a changed node in a diagram and the fill of an entry-point node.

### Secondary
- **Signal Amber** (`signal`): the stroke of the awaiting-review and awaiting-reply rings, the half fill of the first and the centre dot of the second. On the review page it also strokes the broken ring of the stale notice and fills the triangle of the Needs Attention mark. It matches the amber of the logo. It is also the text selection background, with `signal-foreground` ink on it.
- **Signal Amber Text** (`signal-text`): the darker amber that carries the bold "Awaiting review" and "Awaiting reply" status labels at text contrast, and the caret. On the review page it carries the bold label of the drafting strip, the bold message of the stale notice, the Needs Attention label and the label of a pending comment.

### Tertiary
- **Line colours** (`line-blue`, `line-green`, `line-magenta`, `line-violet`, `line-teal`, `line-lime`): one per repository. They colour the roundel, the 4px line, the terminus bar and the solid ring of a merged stop. On the review page the repository's colour draws the section rail, the 2px underline of a code link, the border of the current waypoint, the filled ring of a reviewed toggle, the outline of a hovered or selected diagram node and the control that adds a comment to a line. The light values sit near 53% lightness for contrast on the white panel; the dark values rise to 70 to 78%.
- **Line Foreground** (`line-foreground`): the letter inside a roundel, and the check or plus drawn on a line-coloured fill. White in light mode, panel ink in dark mode.
- **Syntax colours** (`syntax-keyword`, `syntax-string`, `syntax-number`, `syntax-function`, `syntax-type`, `syntax-quiet`): the token colours of highlighted code. Keywords take the violet hue, strings the green, numbers and constants the magenta, functions the blue, types and tags the teal, each darkened to text contrast on the code surface. `syntax-quiet` is a neutral for comments, punctuation and line numbers. Properties and plain tokens stay in the foreground ink.
- **Diff colours** (`diff-added`, `diff-removed`): the bold "+" and "-" in the mark column of a code line, and the tint of the whole line at 12% and 10%. Added shares the green of the line palette and removed shares the error red.

### Neutral
- **Paper** (`background`): the page, and the fill of a waypoint box set into code. It is a buff off-white at 95.5% lightness in light mode, deliberately below white so that fields and code stand out from it, and a warm charcoal at 20% in dark mode.
- **Code Surface** (`code`): the fill behind code lines. It is three steps lighter than the paper in light mode (98.5%) and well below it in dark mode (15.5%), so code reads as its own material.
- **Field White** (`card`): the fill of inputs, outline buttons and control groups such as the waypoint stepper and a board's toolbar. On the review page it is also the fill of a diagram node plate and of a comment thread set into code. It never fills a section.
- **Quiet Fill** (`muted`): the hover fill of a stop row, the hover of outline and ghost buttons, and skeleton shapes. On the review page it is also the file header of a code panel, the row that stands for hidden lines, inline code in prose and the hover of a rail row.
- **Secondary Ink** (`muted-foreground`): sources, details, counts, timestamps, the quiet status labels (stale, draft, closed, merged), the strokes and fills of the quiet rings and the counts in the status filter. On the review page it also draws diagram edges, the nit and question marks, and on a Blast Diagram the plain mark of a file the change did not touch.
- **Sign Secondary** (`sign-muted`): secondary text and quiet strokes on the sign band, such as the quiet pull request status labels, an updated artifact's label, the forge message and a pending reviewer's mark.
- **Hairline** (`border`): the 1px rule between the bands of the review page and between rows, the frame of a code panel and a diagram board, and the dot grid of a board.
- **Field Stroke** (`input`): the 1px border of inputs and outline buttons, and the scrollbar thumb. On the review page it also rules waypoint boxes, comment threads and diagram node plates.
- **Focus Blue** (`ring`): the 2px focus outline, offset 2px, on every focusable element.
- **Error Red** (`destructive`): the error message under a field and the border of an invalid field. On the review page it also marks what is wrong with the change: the bug and risk finding marks, the mark of a file a Blast Diagram shows as breaking, and a reviewer who requested changes.

### Named Rules
**The Rationed Amber Rule.** Amber marks the logo and what waits on the reviewer. On the index page that is the two statuses awaiting review and awaiting reply. On the review page it is five things: the review header's own awaiting-review and awaiting-reply status labels, the drafting strip (the half-amber ring and its bold label), the stale notice (the broken ring drawn with `tone="signal"` and its bold message), the Needs Attention mark with its label, and the label of a pending comment. An out-of-date artifact's label in the header is amber too, for the same reason the stale notice is: both say the artifact is waiting on an update. The broken ring is amber only in the stale notice; on the index page the same ring is secondary ink. Amber is never a link colour, a heading colour or decoration. Selection and caret are its only other appearances. The primary button is filled with Primary Amber, a separate token: a filled rectangle with ink text is an action, and an amber ring or bold amber text is a status, so the two never share a shape.

**The Primary Button Rule.** A primary button is Primary Amber on every page, inside a line or not, including Submit review in the band. It never takes a line colour.

**The Line Text Rule.** Text takes the line colour where it names or locates the line: the repository name at the head of a line, and on the review page the Segment Title, the heading of each band, the segment titles and section labels of the section rail, and an Outward Link. A page heading, which belongs to no line, stays in ink, as do body text, the small headings inside a band and controls.

**The Line Variable Rule.** A component never names a line colour. The backend sends one of six keys, `LINE_COLOR_CLASSES` in `packages/ui/src/lib/line-color.ts` maps the key to a class that sets `--line`, and every part of the line (roundel, rail, terminus, merged ring) reads `--line`. The review page sets `--line` once on its root from the key of the repository it belongs to. A neutral context such as the status filter sets `--line` to the secondary ink.

**The Shape Before Colour Rule.** Every mark differs from its siblings in shape before it differs in colour, and each has a text label beside it. Ring states: hollow, half filled, centre dot, broken, thin, barred, solid. Finding marks: a filled diamond for a bug, a hollow diamond for a risk, a small dot for a nit, a hollow circle for a question, a filled triangle for Needs Attention. Blast file marks: a ringed disc with a plus for an added file, a plain ringed disc for a modified one and a ringed disc with a bar for a deleted one; a disc with a cross for a file that breaks, a half-filled ring for one whose behaviour changes, and a plain dot for every other file the change did not touch. In diagrams a proven connection ends in a filled arrowhead and an assumed one in a hollow arrowhead, in the same tone. Status never depends on hue alone.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Overpass Variable (with ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Overpass Variable (with ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif)
**Label/Mono Font:** Overpass Mono Variable (with ui-monospace, 'SF Mono', Menlo, monospace)

**Character:** A wayfinding grotesk descended from highway signage, with its fixed-width companion. Both are self-hosted through `@fontsource-variable` and imported in `globals.css`. Hierarchy comes from size and from two weights, regular (400) and bold (700).

### Hierarchy
- **Headline** (700, 2.25rem, line-height 2.5rem, tracking -0.02em): the page heading, one per page.
- **Segment Title** (700, 1.5rem, line-height 2rem, tracking -0.015em): the title of a segment on the review page.
- **Title** (700, 1.25rem, line-height 1.75rem): the repository name at the head of a line, the heading of the empty state, and the heading of each section of the review page.
- **Brand** (700, 1.0625rem, line-height 1, tracking -0.01em): the product name in the sign band.
- **Subtitle** (700, 1rem, line-height 1.5rem): the pull request title in the sign band, the title of a diagram, and the heading of a popover or side panel.
- **Body** (400, 1rem, line-height 1.5rem): the title of a stop and explanatory paragraphs, held to a prose measure. The Context paragraph of a segment is set one step up, at 1.0625rem with a 1.75rem line-height.
- **Label** (400, 0.875rem, line-height 1.25rem): sources, details, author names, counts, timestamps, the status filter, hints and button text. On the review page it is the size of written explanation inside a waypoint, a finding, a comment and a side panel, held to 62 characters. Counts and timestamps use tabular figures.
- **Label Strong** (700, 0.875rem): the "Awaiting review" and "Awaiting reply" status labels, field labels, the roundel letter and the primary button. On the review page, the title of a waypoint or a finding and the name of a comment's author.
- **Node Label** (700, 0.8125rem, line-height 1rem): the label of a diagram node. A diagram entry in the section rail uses the same size at regular weight.
- **Control Small** (400, 0.8rem): the text of small controls: the small outline button, the buttons in the sign band, the reviewed toggle and the waypoint stepper.
- **Caption** (400 or 700, 0.75rem, line-height 1rem): the label of a finding mark in bold, and in regular weight a position count, a line range, a time beside a name and the draft tag.
- **Micro** (400, 0.6875rem, line-height 1rem): the smallest labels on a diagram board: a node's kind and file path, an edge label, and on a Blast Diagram a file's label and what the change did to it. The chat panel uses it for a code reference. It appears nowhere else.
- **Code** (Overpass Mono, 400, 0.94em of its context): branch names, forge slugs and filesystem paths, wherever they appear, including a stop title that is a branch name and the path input. Inline code in prose is the same size on the quiet fill.
- **Code Block** (Overpass Mono, 400, 0.8125rem, line-height 1.25rem): lines of code, in a code panel, a code link's preview and a side panel. The same size in bold sets the path in a file header and a symbol name on a diagram node.

### Named Rules
**The Two Weights Rule.** Text is regular or bold. No medium, no semibold, no light, no italics for emphasis.

**The Mono Means Literal Rule.** Fixed-width type marks a literal string: one the user could paste into a terminal (a branch, a slug, a path), a symbol name, or source code itself. The backend marks each label segment and each inline run as plain or code; `LabelSegments` and `Prose` draw it. Numbers, statuses and names stay in the grotesk.

**The Bullet Separator Rule.** Label segments are separated by "•" (U+2022), set in the grotesk with 0.5em on each side. Overpass draws "·" (U+00B7) with no space after it, so the middle dot is not used.

## Layout

A page is set on the column or at full width. On the column, one centred column with a maximum width of 64rem holds both the contents of the sign band and the page, with 1rem gutters that widen to 1.5rem from 640px; the page starts 2rem below the band and ends with 4rem of space. A page that asks for the full width, as the review page does, gets a band and a page that both run edge to edge with 1.5rem gutters, and sets its own vertical spacing. The route declares the choice and the root layout passes it to the sign band.

Every repository section uses the same two-column grid: a 2.5rem rail column, then the content. The roundel, the 4px line, every ring and the terminus are centred in the rail column, so all lines on a page share one registration and all text shares one left edge. The line is continuous from the roundel through the last stop; the segment below the last ring is hidden.

A repository section opens with a 2px ink rule, with 1.25rem above its heading and 1.5rem below its last stop. The heading row carries the name and source at the left, and the count and the "Create Flight Plan" control at the right. A stop row is a single link with 0.625rem of vertical padding; from 640px it is four columns (title and details, an 8rem author column, an 8rem status column, a 7.5rem right-aligned time column). Below 640px the author, status and time move under the details on one line, and the heading row wraps so the count and control sit under the source.

A line with no stops, or with a forge problem, ends in a terminus: a short line segment closed by a 4px horizontal bar, with the message beside it. The review page uses the same terminus where a review target has no review artifact.

The page heading row holds the heading, the summary label on its baseline and the primary action at the far right. The status filter button sits right-aligned in the row below and wraps under the notice on narrow screens.

The review page is built for desktop widths. Notice rows run the full width under the band, each closed by a hairline: the stale notice, then the drafting strip. Below them a two-column grid holds a 13rem section rail and the content, 2rem apart. The rail is sticky under the band and scrolls on its own when it is taller than the window. The content column stacks the segments. Each segment is a Segment Title, then its bands in a fixed order: Context, Journey Flow Diagrams, Blast Diagram, Code Walkthrough. Bands are separated by hairlines, with 1.5rem above a band's heading and 2rem below its content. The pull request's Conversation is the last band. Written explanation is held to 62 characters and lists of pairs to 44rem, while code panels and diagram boards take the full width of the column.

Three layers stay in view while the review page scrolls. The sign band is first. The heading row of the Code Walkthrough, 2.75rem tall, pins under the band and carries the waypoint stepper, the reviewed switch and the waypoint filter. The 2.25rem header of the file being read pins under that. Jumping to a section or a waypoint stops it below these layers.

The chat panel docks at the right edge under the band, 26rem wide, and the page gives up that width while it is open.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is flat. There are no shadows. A menu, a dialog, a popover, a code link's preview, the selection toolbar and a toast open over the page with a 1px ink border and no shadow. Depth comes from two things only: the ink sign band against the panel, and hairline rules. The band is sticky and stays above the page while it scrolls; the pinned rows of the review page sit under it on the panel colour with a hairline below. Hover is a tonal fill, not a lift.

### Named Rules
**The Hairline Rule.** Sections are divided by a 1px rule in the hairline colour. The Line Head Rule and the Segment Break Rule state the two heavier rules. Content is never wrapped in a bordered, filled or shadowed container. The Framed Code Rule states the one exception.

**The Framed Code Rule.** On the review page each file's code panel and each diagram board is framed by a 1px hairline border with 0.3rem corners. The page keeps the layout of the legacy review panel, and code sits on its own `code` surface, so the frame marks where that surface starts and ends. What is set into the code is ruled in the field stroke: a waypoint box, a comment thread and a comment composer. A node on a board is a ruled plate. Nothing else is framed, and no frame takes a shadow.

**The Line Head Rule.** Every repository section opens with a 2px rule in the ink colour, on the index page and on the repositories step of setup, as does the empty state. It is the rule a timetable draws above each line's block.

**The Segment Break Rule.** One segment is separated from the next by a 4px rule in the ink colour, the weight of the line. It is the heaviest horizontal rule in the system, and the first segment has none.

**The Tonal Depth Rule.** Surfaces separate by lightness, not by shadow. In light mode the paper is 95.5%, the quiet fill 91.5%, the code surface 98.5% and field white 99.5%. In dark mode the paper is 20%, the quiet fill 27.5%, fields 25% and the code surface 15.5%, with the sign band below all of them at 13%. Borders are opaque warm greys, not translucent white.

## Shapes

Two shape families. The diagram is drawn with circles and straight strokes: a 1.75rem round roundel, a 1.25rem ring with a 3px stroke, a 4px line and a 4px terminus bar. Controls are small rectangles with slightly eased corners: 0.375rem on buttons and inputs, 0.3rem on small buttons, 0.225rem on the stop row's hover fill and focus outline and on skeleton shapes. Borders are 1px. Nothing is pill-shaped except the roundel.

The review page adds to the first family and keeps the second. The section rail marks a segment with a square in the line colour, 13px with softened corners, so a segment reads apart from the rings of its sections. Finding marks are a diamond, a dot, a circle and a triangle at 0.875rem. Blast file marks are discs of 5px to 14px radius. Diagram edges are 2px strokes that end in a small arrowhead; the imports of a Blast Diagram are 1.25px. Framed panels, boards, waypoint boxes and node plates take 0.3rem corners; popovers and the toast take 0.375rem. A changed node carries a 2px ink border in place of the 1px field stroke.

## Components

### Sign Band
The fixed sign at the top of every page. Full-width ink, 3.5rem tall, sticky. Its `width` is `column`, where its contents sit on the 64rem page column, or `full`, where they run edge to edge with 1.5rem gutters. A review page draws a Band Line in it: a 4px rule in the repository's line colour along the band's lower edge, inside its 3.5rem, so the band says which line the reader is on. The setup page draws it in Primary Amber. The index page, which shows every line, leaves the band plain. It carries the brand mark at the left, linked to the homepage. The rest of the band is a slot that a page fills with its own header; the slot is divided from the brand mark by a 1px rule at 20% of the band's foreground and collapses when the page puts nothing in it. The brand mark is the 32px logo (`logo-small.png`, the heavier small-size cut, also the favicon) and the product name in the Brand style, 0.625rem apart. `logo.png` is the faithful amber recolour for larger sizes.

### Review Header
The header of the review page, drawn inside the sign band at full width. For a pull request it reads left to right: the author's Avatar at 1.75rem, the title in Subtitle truncated to one line, the pull request's Stop Ring and status label (the same mark as the index page, in the band's own colours), and, when the review artifact has one, its freshness label — "Updated" in the band's secondary colour, or "Out of date" in bold amber. On a review target with no review artifact, the count of changed files by kind follows the status as Label Segments in the band's secondary colour, with tabular figures. Amber labels on the band use `signal`, not `signal-text`, because the band is ink in both themes. At the right are the reviewers' Avatars at 1.5rem, then Chat, Share, Submit review and the Options Menu. For a branch with no forge it shows a branch icon, the branch name in bold mono, the artifact's freshness label when there is one, the forge message in the band's secondary colour and one link.

Each reviewer's Avatar carries a small round mark at its lower right that differs by shape: empty for pending, a speech mark for commented, a check for approved, a cross on error red for changes requested. The state is also given as text to assistive technology and in a tooltip.

Controls in the band are band buttons: 1.75rem tall with 0.3rem corners, Control Small text, a 1px border at 35% of the band's foreground and a 10% fill on hover. The Options Menu is the same button narrowed to an icon. Chat is a toggle that inverts to a light fill with ink text while the chat panel is open. Submit review is the one filled button in the band, Primary Amber with bold ink text. The Options Menu, Share and Submit review open a popover or menu below the band.

### Options Menu
A band button narrowed to an Ellipsis icon, the last control in the band, after Submit review. Its menu holds "View pull request" (external, when there is one), "Update the Flight Plan", "Regenerate from scratch", a hairline, then "Delete" in error red. ### Section Rail
The review page's navigation, in a 13rem column at the left. It reuses the 2.5rem rail column and the 4px line in the repository's colour. Each segment is a row with a square in the line colour, the segment's title in bold in the line colour and its detail in Caption below. Section labels are set in the line colour too. Under it the segment's sections are rings on the line: a 1.25rem ring with a 3px secondary-ink stroke and a panel fill. A diagram inside a section is a smaller ring with a 2px stroke in the line colour and a label at 0.8125rem in secondary ink. The ring of the section in view fills with the line colour and its label turns bold; the small ring of a diagram in view fills the same way. The Conversation follows a hairline at the end, on its own short line. Fill and stroke change at 180ms ease-out. A row takes the quiet fill on hover and jumps to its section.

The rail is how the line stays a gutter marker on this page. It shows position and nothing else: no ring in it carries a status, and no content sits on the line.

### Line Roundel
A 1.75rem circle filled with the line colour, holding one bold letter in the line foreground. The letter comes from the backend. It is decorative for assistive technology; the repository name beside it is the heading.

### Stop Ring
The signature component. A 1.25rem ring centred on the line, in one state per branch status:
- **no-review**: hollow. Panel fill, 3px secondary-ink stroke.
- **awaiting-review**: half amber. Panel fill, amber stroke, the right half filled amber.
- **awaiting-reply**: amber ring with an amber centre dot.
- **stale**: broken. Panel fill, secondary-ink stroke dashed into short segments.
- **draft**: thin. Panel fill, 1.5px secondary-ink stroke.
- **closed**: solid secondary ink with a panel-coloured bar across it.
- **merged**: solid. Fill and stroke in the line colour.

A state change transitions fill, stroke, stroke width, dash pattern and the opacity of the inner marks at 180ms ease-out. The ring keeps its size and position in every state, so nothing reflows. The status filter reuses the same component at 1rem with a neutral line colour.

The `tone` prop is `status` by default, which draws each state as listed. `tone="signal"` draws the stroke in amber whatever the state. The review page's stale notice uses it on the broken ring, and that is its only use.

### Review Target Stop
One row per review target, and the whole row is one link. The title is in Body, the details in Label with bullet separators, the author as an Avatar and a name in Label, the status label in Label (bold amber text for the two awaiting statuses, foreground for no review, secondary ink for the rest) and the time in secondary ink with tabular figures.
- **Hover:** a quiet fill appears behind the row, extended 0.75rem past the row on each side, with 0.225rem corners, at 180ms ease-out.
- **Focus:** a 2px focus-blue outline on the same extended shape.

### Avatar
A 1.25rem circle with a 1px field stroke. It shows the person's picture, or their monogram in bold at 0.625rem on field white when there is no picture or the picture fails to load. The backend sends the picture URL and the monogram. It is decorative for assistive technology; the name beside it carries the meaning. A review target with no author, such as a local branch, leaves the author column empty. The review page draws it at 1.5rem beside a comment and for a reviewer, and at 1.75rem for the author in the sign band, where its stroke and fill take the band's foreground.

### Filter Menu
A small outline button that opens a menu of tickable options. The button label, sent by the backend, says how much is selected ("Status: 4 of 7") and ends in a chevron. Each option is a row with a checkbox, an optional marker, a label and a right-aligned count. Ticking an option keeps the menu open. A reset row follows a hairline when the selection is not the default. The menu has 0.375rem corners, a 1px ink border, the popover fill and no shadow; the focused row takes the quiet fill.

The status filter is a Filter Menu whose markers are Stop Rings, so it is also the key to the rings. Draft, closed and merged are unticked by default. The selection lives in the URL. Counts always describe the whole list, and the summary and each line's count read "5 of 7 review targets" whenever some are hidden. A line whose review targets are all hidden stays listed and says how many are hidden.

The author filter is a Filter Menu whose markers are Avatars. It depends on the status filter: it lists only the authors of review targets that have a selected status, with counts for those, then "No author" for review targets without one. All are ticked by default. Its button reads "Author: all" or "Author: 3 of 5". The two filters sit side by side, status first, and combine: a review target shows when it passes both.

The waypoint filter of the Code Walkthrough is a Filter Menu whose markers are Finding Marks, so it is also the key to those marks. Its selection lives in the URL.

Full-width rows under the sign band of the review page, each in Label with 0.625rem of vertical padding and a hairline below.

### Code Link

### Diagram Board
The framed surface a diagram is drawn on, with 0.3rem corners and a 16px dot grid in the hairline colour. A control group at its top right zooms out, zooms in, resets the zoom and opens full screen. ### Diagram Nodes and Edges
A node is a flat ruled plate: field white, a field stroke, 0.3rem corners, the Node Label and Micro lines for its type and path. A node inside another is drawn inside its plate, on the muted field at every other depth, and a trigger label is a ruled row inside its node with its icon, its label and the name of its code in mono Micro. A listed block of a node (an argument, a state, a hook, an effect or a step) is a row of the same kind, with the name of its code in mono secondary ink, under a Micro heading in bold uppercase secondary ink that names its kind. A node the change touches carries a 2px ink border. A node whose text is not written yet shows its symbol name in mono secondary ink. Hovering or selecting a node draws a 2px outline in the line colour at 180ms ease-out.

Edges are 2px strokes in secondary ink. Every edge of the Journey Flow Diagram is one solid line with a filled arrowhead that carries no label. Between two columns that follow each other it is straight, from the right edge of one node towards the left edge of the next, and it bends 24px before the node so that the arrowhead arrives level. The edges that reach the same head or row arrive 18px apart, closer where the row has no room for that. An edge that passes a column crosses it level, in a lane between the nodes of that column, and the edges that pass a column to the same node run side by side, 18px apart, never as one line. A node is set level with the row or the head its edge leaves.

### Blast Diagram
A graph of files on a Diagram Board, with no plates, zones or columns. The change is at the centre of the board, and the board opens centred on it: the files of the change are innermost, the deepest in the import graph at the very centre, and every other file is further out the more imports lie between it and the change. A file is a Blast File Mark with its label under it in mono Micro on the panel colour, and under that, for a file of the change, a file that breaks or a file whose behaviour changes, what the mark says in Micro secondary ink. A file that is unaffected, not assessed or not reached is drawn plain, with nothing under its label, and its label is secondary ink; every other file's is foreground. A file is drawn larger the more files it is connected to. An import is a straight 1.25px stroke in secondary ink at 55% opacity, from the rim of the file that imports to the rim of the file it imports, ending in a small filled arrowhead.

Hovering, focusing or selecting a file keeps it and the files joined to it: its label turns bold, the labels of the joined files and the imports between them turn to foreground at full strength, and every other file drops to 25% opacity and every other import to 15%, at 180ms ease-out. Selecting a file opens the board's side panel with the path as its title in bold mono and where the file stands above the title (Added, Breaks, Unaffected, Not assessed and so on), then its impact reason as Prose, or one fixed sentence in secondary ink for a plain file, the files that import it and the files it imports as rows in mono that select that file, and a small outline button to its File Section when it has one.

Above the board two rows in Label: the counts as Label Segments, then the key in secondary ink, with each Blast File Mark the diagram draws beside its label and an arrow beside what it points at.

### Blast File Mark
The mark of a file on a Blast Diagram, always with a label. A file of the change is a disc inside a 1.5px ring, 3px out, in the colour of what the diff did to it. A file the change did not touch has no ring.
- **added**: green (`diff-added`), with a plus in the panel colour.
- **modified**: blue (`line-blue`), with nothing on the disc.
- **deleted**: red (`diff-removed`), with a bar in the panel colour.
- **breaks**: a disc in error red with a cross in the panel colour.
- **behaviour-changes**: an ink ring with its right half filled.
- **plain**: a plain disc in secondary ink, for a file that is unaffected, not assessed or not reached by the change.

The three colours of a changed file are the only place a Blast Diagram uses the blue of the line palette as a fixed colour: there it means modified, whatever the repository's line colour is.

The agents are rows divided by hairlines, as on the setup page: a radio, the agent's name in bold and its description in Label secondary ink. A picked agent opens its fields under its name: its model as a Select Field, 15rem wide, with its effort beside it as a Segmented Choice. An agent that cannot be started keeps its row, with its radio disabled and the reason beside a bold "Unavailable.". A rejection about one field is shown under that field in error red. A hairline closes the form, with the primary action at the right; its label follows the chosen agent.

Starting the review builds the artifact's structure and opens it as a drafting review, so the form has no waiting state of its own. When the change cannot be read, the reason is shown above the action in error red.

### Suggestion Field
A text field that suggests matches while the user types: the repository folder search and the user search of the Add repository dialog and of the setup page. It follows the field style: a bold Label, a hint in secondary ink, then the field. The suggestions open under the field in a list with 0.375rem corners, a 1px ink border, the popover fill and no shadow. Each suggestion is one row in Label: an optional marker such as an Avatar, the label, a detail in secondary ink, and a state label at the right. The row the arrow keys are on takes the quiet fill. A suggestion that cannot be chosen, such as a folder that is already added, is set in secondary ink with its state label. When nothing matches, the list holds one message in secondary ink. A field that takes a path sets the typed text and its suggestions in the mono face, and its placeholder in the grotesk.

### Setup Route
The left rail of the setup page, 17rem wide and sticky under the band. It reuses the 2.5rem rail column and the 4px line. Setup belongs to no repository, so its line is drawn in Primary Amber and never in a line colour: the rail, the Band Line and the solid ring of a saved step are `primary`, and the names of the saved and current steps, the step's Segment Title and its section headings are amber at text contrast (`signal-text`). The rail has no roundel and runs from the first stop to the last. Each of the four steps is a stop of the same height with its name in Body and, in Label secondary ink, what the step is for or what was saved in it. The Stop Ring says where the step stands: thin for a step not reached, hollow for the step in hand with its name in bold, and solid for a saved step. A saved step's name is a link back to it, underlined in the field stroke. Saving a step changes its ring in place at 180ms ease-out and no stop moves.

The page opens with its Headline, "Welcome to Flight Plan", and one paragraph in secondary ink above the rail and the step. The step itself sits at the right of the rail: its name as a Segment Title, one paragraph held to 62 characters, then the options as rows divided by hairlines. A row is a checkbox or a radio, the option's name in bold and its description in Label secondary ink; a picked option opens its fields under its name. An option that cannot be used keeps its row, with its control disabled and the reason beside a bold "Unavailable.". The row that makes one option the default is a radio at the right end of the row. A hairline closes the step, with a ghost "Back" at the left and the primary action at the right. A rejection that is about one field is shown under that field; any other rejection is shown above the actions.

On the repositories step each added repository is a section drawn as on the index page: the roundel and the line in its line colour, its name as a Title and its path in mono. On local git, which cannot tell who the user is, the first stop asks the user to pick themselves from the people who committed: a hollow ring beside a Suggestion Field of its own, which becomes a solid ring with the Avatar, the name, a detail and a bold "You" once chosen. Each tracked user is a solid ring on the line with an Avatar, the name and a detail. The line ends in a terminus that holds the Suggestion Field for tracking another user. With no repository yet, a terminus in secondary ink carries the message.

### Loading, empty and error states
The skeleton draws the page's own structure in quiet fill: a heading bar, then lines with a roundel, a rail and stop bars, under hairlines. It pulses only when motion is allowed. The empty state is a Title and a paragraph under a hairline, held to a prose measure. A route error is a Headline, the backend's message in secondary ink and a primary "Try again" button.

The review page's skeleton draws a notice row, the rail column and the first band. A review target with no review artifact shows a terminus in the line colour with a Title and a paragraph, then the review request form described under Review Request Form. A segment with no Blast Diagram has no Blast Diagram band, and no entry for it in the section rail. A segment with no Journey Flow Diagram shows the band's heading alone.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** draw status with a Stop Ring and a text label together.
- **Do** set `--line` through `LINE_COLOR_CLASSES` from the key the backend sends, and read `--line` everywhere a line colour is drawn.
- **Do** keep the 2.5rem rail column and the 4px line weight on every surface that shows a line, so all lines register.
- **Do** separate sections with a 1px hairline and space, open each repository's line with the 2px ink rule, and separate one segment from the next with the 4px ink rule.
- **Do** keep every neutral warm, and separate surfaces by the lightness steps of the Tonal Depth Rule.
- **Do** frame a file's code panel and a diagram board with a 1px hairline border, and rule what is set into the code with the field stroke.
- **Do** give every new mark its own shape, and a text label beside it.
- **Do** draw a diagram edge solid for a call, dashed for a value and dotted for data, with a filled arrowhead when the connection is proven and a hollow one when it is assumed.
- **Do** put a page's header in the sign band's slot, and ask for the full width when the page needs it.
- **Do** set branch names, forge slugs, paths, symbol names and code in Overpass Mono, and everything else in Overpass.
- **Do** join label segments with "•" (U+2022).
- **Do** centre single-line text in controls, avatars and roundels by its capital height with the `text-box-cap` utility; Overpass sits high in its line box otherwise.
- **Do** transition state in place at 180ms ease-out, and hold decorative pulsing behind the reduced-motion preference.
- **Do** render the labels, counts, colours and order the backend sends.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use amber outside the logo, the primary button and what waits on the reviewer: the awaiting-review and awaiting-reply statuses (on the index page and in the review header), and on the review page the drafting strip, the stale notice, an out-of-date artifact's label, the Needs Attention mark and a pending comment's label.
- **Don't** wrap content in cards, or add shadows. Code panels and diagram boards are the only framed content. Menus, popovers and dialogs are the overlays, and they are set off by a 1px ink border.
- **Don't** use a third weight, or a third typeface.
- **Don't** use "·" (U+00B7) as a separator; Overpass draws it with no space after it.
- **Don't** show status as a coloured badge or pill; the ring is the status mark.
- **Don't** tell two marks, two edges or two arrowheads apart by colour alone.
- **Don't** change the size or position of a ring or a row when its state changes.
- **Don't** choose a line colour, compute a count or build a label in a component.
- **Don't** redraw the review page as a line diagram; there the line is a gutter marker only. The section rail is that marker: it draws the line, the segment squares and the section rings in the gutter to show where the reader is, and the review's content stays in bands beside it.
