# Design System: Flight Plan

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Line Diagram"**

Flight Plan reads like transit signage. Every repository is a line with its own colour and a lettered roundel, and every review target is a stop on that line. The status of a review artifact is drawn in the stop's ring, so it reads before any word does. An ink sign band runs across the top of every page and carries the amber logo and the product name; a page puts its own header in the band, as the review page does.

The panels below the band are warm timetable paper in light mode and warm charcoal in dark mode, chosen by `prefers-color-scheme`. Structure comes from ruled lines and one shared column registration, not from boxes. Depth is tonal: the paper, the code surface, the fields and the quiet fill sit at clearly different lightness, so each reads as its own material without a shadow. The density is that of a timetable: compact rows, generous space between lines, nothing floating. The system replaces the sidebar, table and badge dashboard with the diagram itself.

The frontend only renders. Labels, counts, line colours, roundel letters, monograms, the status filter, the filtering itself, highlighted code tokens, diagram positions and the order of everything come from the backend; components pick a variant from a discriminant prop and draw it.

The review page keeps the layout of the legacy review panel: one full-width page read top to bottom, with the review header in the sign band, a section rail in the left gutter, and each segment's Context Section, Trace Diagrams, Blast Diagram and Code Walkthrough in order at its right. The line appears there only as a gutter marker. The rail draws it to show where the reader is, and the content beside it is set in ruled bands, framed code panels and diagram boards, not as stops on a line.

**Key Characteristics:**
- A 4px vertical line per repository, in that repository's colour, with stops as rings on it.
- Seven ring states, one per branch status, each a different shape.
- An ink sign band, 56px tall, sticky at the top of every page, on the page column or at full width. On a review it carries a 4px rule in the repository's line colour along its lower edge.
- A 2px ink rule at the head of every line, hairline rules between sections; no cards, no shadows. Only code panels and diagram boards are framed.
- Marks that differ by shape before colour: stop rings, finding marks, blast file marks, edge strokes and arrowheads.
- One grotesk at two weights, with its fixed-width companion for code and code-like text.
- Amber is the primary colour: it fills every primary button, with ink text, and marks what waits on the reviewer, as a ring and a bold label.
- Text in the line colour where it names or locates the line: a repository's name, and on a review its headings and the labels of the section rail.
- State changes in place at 180ms ease-out; nothing reflows.

## Colors

Warm paper and a warm near-black ink carry six saturated line colours and one rationed amber. Every neutral is tinted toward the paper, at hue 60 to 90; none is a pure grey or a blue slate. Code sits on its own surface, with syntax colours that take their hues from the line palette. All values are OKLCH custom properties in `packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css`; each token has a light value and a `-dark` sibling in the frontmatter, and the stylesheet switches between them by `prefers-color-scheme`.

### Primary
- **Sign Ink** (`sign`, `foreground`): the sign band and body text share one ink in light mode. In dark mode the band drops to a deeper ink than the panel. A control that is switched on inverts to this ink.
- **Primary Amber** (`primary`): Flight Plan's primary colour. It fills every primary button, with ink text (`primary-foreground`) in both themes, as on airport wayfinding, and tints ticked checkboxes and radios through `accent-color`. It is the logo's amber at button strength, brighter than `signal`. The same ink draws the 4px rule between segments, the 2px border of a changed node in a diagram and the fill of an entry-point node.

### Secondary
- **Signal Amber** (`signal`): the stroke of the awaiting-review and awaiting-reply rings, the half fill of the first and the centre dot of the second. On the review page it also strokes the broken ring of the stale notice and fills the triangle of the Core change mark. It matches the amber of the logo. It is also the text selection background, with `signal-foreground` ink on it.
- **Nit Blue** (`nit`): the thin ring of a nit finding, with the short stroke inside it, and its label. A sky blue between the teal and blue line colours, so a nit reads before the code beside it without borrowing the red of a bug or the rationed amber.
- **Risk Yellow** (`risk`, `risk-text`): the ring and the diamond of a risk finding, and its label in `risk-text`, which is darker in light mode for text contrast. It is a lighter, greener yellow than Signal Amber, so a risk and the Core change mark beside it differ in tone as well as in shape.
- **Waypoint Blue** (`waypoint`, Line Blue): the waypoint lane's own colour. It draws the ring and centre dot of the current waypoint's stop, the halo around a current stop that carries a finding, the solid disc of a reviewed stop and the Reviewed mark of the waypoint filter, and the stem of a current or open waypoint. The lane never takes the line amber, which belongs to Core change.
- **Signal Amber Text** (`signal-text`): the darker amber that carries the bold "Awaiting review" and "Awaiting reply" status labels at text contrast, and the caret. On the review page it carries the bold label of the drafting strip, the bold message of the stale notice, the Core change label and the label of a pending comment.

### Tertiary
- **Line colours** (`line-blue`, `line-green`, `line-magenta`, `line-violet`, `line-teal`, `line-lime`): one per repository. They colour the roundel, the 4px line, the terminus bar and the solid ring of a merged stop. On the review page the repository's colour draws the section rail, the 2px underline of a code link, the border of the current waypoint, the filled ring of a reviewed toggle, the outline of a hovered or selected diagram node, and the control that adds a comment to a line. The light values sit near 53% lightness for contrast on the white panel; the dark values rise to 70 to 78%.
- **Line Foreground** (`line-foreground`): the letter inside a roundel, and the check or plus drawn on a line-coloured fill. White in light mode, panel ink in dark mode.
- **Syntax colours** (`syntax-keyword`, `syntax-string`, `syntax-number`, `syntax-function`, `syntax-type`, `syntax-quiet`): the token colours of highlighted code. Keywords take the violet hue, strings the green, numbers and constants the magenta, functions the blue, types and tags the teal, each darkened to text contrast on the code surface. `syntax-quiet` is a neutral for comments, punctuation and line numbers. Properties and plain tokens stay in the foreground ink.
- **Diff colours** (`diff-added`, `diff-removed`): the bold "+" and "-" in the mark column of a code line, and the tint of the whole line at 12% and 10%. Added shares the green of the line palette and removed shares the error red.

### Neutral
- **Paper** (`background`): the page, and the fill of a waypoint box set into code. It is a buff off-white at 95.5% lightness in light mode, deliberately below white so that fields and code stand out from it, and a warm charcoal at 20% in dark mode.
- **Code Surface** (`code`): the fill behind code lines. It is three steps lighter than the paper in light mode (98.5%) and well below it in dark mode (15.5%), so code reads as its own material.
- **Field White** (`card`): the fill of inputs, outline buttons and control groups such as the view controls at the top right of a diagram board. On the review page it is also the fill of a diagram node plate and of a comment thread set into code. It never fills a section.
- **Quiet Fill** (`muted`): the hover fill of a stop row, the hover of outline and ghost buttons, and skeleton shapes. On the review page it is also the file header of a code panel, the row that stands for hidden lines, inline code in prose and the hover of a rail row.
- **Secondary Ink** (`muted-foreground`): sources, details, counts, timestamps, the quiet status labels (stale, draft, closed, merged), the strokes and fills of the quiet rings and the counts in the status filter. On the review page it also draws diagram edges, the question mark, and on a Blast Diagram the plain mark of a file the change did not touch.
- **Sign Secondary** (`sign-muted`): secondary text and quiet strokes on the sign band, such as the quiet pull request status labels, an updated artifact's label, the forge message and a pending reviewer's mark.
- **Hairline** (`border`): the 1px rule between the bands of the review page and between rows, the frame of a code panel and a diagram board, and the dot grid of a board.
- **Field Stroke** (`input`): the 1px border of inputs and outline buttons, and the scrollbar thumb. On the review page it also rules waypoint boxes, comment threads and diagram node plates.
- **Focus Blue** (`ring`): the 2px focus outline, offset 2px, on every focusable element.
- **Error Red** (`destructive`): the error message under a field and the border of an invalid field. On the review page it also marks what is wrong with the change: the bug finding mark, the mark of a file a Blast Diagram shows as breaking, and a reviewer who requested changes.

### Named Rules
**The Rationed Amber Rule.** Amber marks the logo and what waits on the reviewer. On the index page that is the two statuses awaiting review and awaiting reply. On the review page it is five things: the review header's own awaiting-review and awaiting-reply status labels, the drafting strip (the half-amber ring and its bold label), the stale notice (the broken ring drawn with `tone="signal"` and its bold message), the Core change mark with its label, and the label of a pending comment. An out-of-date artifact's label in the header is amber too, for the same reason the stale notice is: both say the artifact is waiting on an update. The broken ring is amber only in the stale notice; on the index page the same ring is secondary ink. Amber is never a link colour, a heading colour or decoration. Selection and caret are its only other appearances. The primary button is filled with Primary Amber, a separate token: a filled rectangle with ink text is an action, and an amber ring or bold amber text is a status, so the two never share a shape.

**The Primary Button Rule.** A primary button is Primary Amber on every page, inside a line or not, including Submit review in the band. It never takes a line colour.

**The Line Text Rule.** Text takes the line colour where it names or locates the line: the repository name at the head of a line, and on the review page the Segment Title, the heading of each band, the segment titles and section labels of the section rail, and an Outward Link. A page heading, which belongs to no line, stays in ink, as do body text, the small headings inside a band and controls.

**The Line Variable Rule.** A component never names a line colour. The backend sends one of six keys, `LINE_COLOR_CLASSES` in `packages/ui/src/lib/line-color.ts` maps the key to a class that sets `--line`, and every part of the line (roundel, rail, terminus, merged ring) reads `--line`. The review page sets `--line` once on its root from the key of the repository it belongs to. A neutral context such as the status filter sets `--line` to the secondary ink.

**The Shape Before Colour Rule.** Every mark differs from its siblings in shape before it differs in colour, and each has a text label beside it. Ring states: hollow, half filled, centre dot, broken, thin, barred, solid. Finding marks are stops: a red disc with a diamond cut out for a bug, a yellow ring with a diamond inside for a risk, a thin blue ring with a stroke for a nit, an ink ring with a question mark for a question, an amber disc with a triangle cut out for Core change. In the gutter a waypoint with no finding is a plain ring, and the Spotlight Mark is four rays around a stop. Thread marks: a filled speech mark for an open thread, a thin dashed one for a resolved thread and a hollow amber one with a centre dot for a comment not sent yet. A gutter mark has no label beside it: its name is read to assistive technology and shown in its preview. Blast file marks: a ringed disc with a plus for an added file, a plain ringed disc for a modified one and a ringed disc with a bar for a deleted one; a disc with a cross for a file that breaks, a half-filled ring for one whose behaviour changes, and a plain dot for every other file the change did not touch. Field marks: a filled square for a field that was changed, a hollow square for an unchanged one and a hollow dashed square for a field of a section that is off; the label of a field mark is read to assistive technology on its entry and written once in the key under the Field Index. In diagrams a proven connection ends in a filled arrowhead and an assumed one in a hollow arrowhead, in the same tone. Status never depends on hue alone.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Overpass Variable (with ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Overpass Variable (with ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif)
**Label/Mono Font:** Overpass Mono Variable (with ui-monospace, 'SF Mono', Menlo, monospace)

**Character:** A wayfinding grotesk descended from highway signage, with its fixed-width companion. Both are self-hosted through `@fontsource-variable` and imported in `globals.css`. Hierarchy comes from size and from two weights, regular (400) and bold (700).

### Hierarchy
- **Headline** (700, 2.25rem, line-height 2.5rem, tracking -0.02em): the page heading, one per page.
- **Segment Title** (700, 1.5rem, line-height 2rem, tracking -0.015em): the title of a segment on the review page.
- **Title** (700, 1.25rem, line-height 1.75rem): the repository name at the head of a line, the heading of the empty state, and the heading of each section of the review page.
- **Brand** (700, 1.0625rem, line-height 1, tracking -0.01em): the product name in the sign band.
- **Subtitle** (700, 1rem, line-height 1.5rem): the pull request title in the sign band, the title of a diagram, and the heading of a popover or side panel.
- **Body** (400, 1rem, line-height 1.5rem): the title of a stop and explanatory paragraphs, held to a prose measure. The Context paragraph of a segment is set one step up, at 1.0625rem with a 1.75rem line-height.
- **Label** (400, 0.875rem, line-height 1.25rem): sources, details, author names, counts, timestamps, the status filter, hints and button text. On the review page it is the size of written explanation inside a waypoint, a finding, a comment and a side panel, held to 62 characters. Counts and timestamps use tabular figures.
- **Label Strong** (700, 0.875rem): the "Awaiting review" and "Awaiting reply" status labels, field labels, the roundel letter and the primary button. On the review page, the title of a waypoint or a finding and the name of a comment's author.
- **Node Label** (700, 0.8125rem, line-height 1rem): the label of a diagram node. A diagram entry in the section rail uses the same size at regular weight.
- **Control Small** (400, 0.8rem): the text of small controls: the small outline button, the buttons in the sign band, the reviewed toggle and the buttons of the Review Navigator.
- **Caption** (400 or 700, 0.75rem, line-height 1rem): the label of a finding mark in bold, and in regular weight a position count, a line range, a time beside a name and the draft tag.
- **Micro** (400, 0.6875rem, line-height 1rem): the smallest labels on a diagram board: a node's kind and file path, an edge label, and on a Blast Diagram a file's label and what the change did to it. The chat panel uses it for a code reference. It appears nowhere else.
- **Code** (Overpass Mono, 400, 0.94em of its context): branch names, forge slugs and filesystem paths, wherever they appear, including a stop title that is a branch name and the path input. Inline code in prose is the same size on the quiet fill.
- **Code Block** (Overpass Mono, 400, 0.8125rem, line-height 1.25rem): lines of code, in a code panel, a code link's preview and a side panel. The same size in bold sets the path in a file header and a symbol name on a diagram node.

### Named Rules
**The Two Weights Rule.** Text is regular or bold. No medium, no semibold, no light, no italics for emphasis.

**The Mono Means Literal Rule.** Fixed-width type marks a literal string: one the user could paste into a terminal (a branch, a slug, a path), a symbol name, or source code itself. The backend marks each label segment and each inline run as plain or code; `LabelSegments` and `Prose` draw it. Numbers, statuses and names stay in the grotesk.

**The Bullet Separator Rule.** Label segments are separated by "•" (U+2022), set in the grotesk with 0.5em on each side. Overpass draws "·" (U+00B7) with no space after it, so the middle dot is not used.

## Layout

A page is set on the column or at full width. On the column, one centred column with a maximum width of 64rem holds both the contents of the sign band and the page, with 1rem gutters that widen to 1.5rem from 640px; the page starts 2rem below the band and ends with 4rem of space. A page that asks for the full width, as the review page does, gets a band and a page that both run edge to edge with 1.5rem gutters, and sets its own vertical spacing. The route declares the choice and the root layout passes it to the sign band.

Every repository section uses the same two-column grid: a 2.5rem rail column, then the content. The roundel, the 4px line, every ring and the terminus are centred in the rail column, so all lines on a page share one registration and all text shares one left edge. The line is continuous from the roundel through the last stop; the segment below the last ring is hidden.

A repository section opens with a 2px ink rule, with 1.25rem above its heading and 1.5rem below its last stop. ## Elevation & Depth

The system is flat. There are no shadows. A menu, a dialog, a popover, a code link's preview, the selection toolbar and a toast open over the page with a 1px ink border and no shadow. A Guide Tooltip is the one overlay drawn in sign ink, with a hairline only in dark mode, so it never reads as part of the page. Depth comes from two things only: the ink sign band against the panel, and hairline rules. The band is sticky and stays above the page while it scrolls; the pinned rows of the review page sit under it on the panel colour with a hairline below. The action row of a Field Panel stays at the foot of the window the same way, on the paper colour with a hairline above. Hover is a tonal fill, not a lift.

### Named Rules
**The Hairline Rule.** Sections are divided by a 1px rule in the hairline colour. The Line Head Rule and the Segment Break Rule state the two heavier rules. Content is never wrapped in a bordered, filled or shadowed container. The Framed Code Rule states the one exception.

**The Framed Code Rule.** On the review page each file's code panel and each diagram board is framed by a 1px hairline border with 0.3rem corners. The page keeps the layout of the legacy review panel, and code sits on its own `code` surface, so the frame marks where that surface starts and ends. What is set into the code is ruled in the field stroke: a waypoint box, a comment thread and a comment composer. A node on a board is a ruled plate. Nothing else is framed, and no frame takes a shadow.

**The Line Head Rule.** Every repository section on the index page opens with a 2px rule in the ink colour, as does the empty state. Each group of the configurations list opens with the same rule. It is the rule a timetable draws above each line's block.

**The Segment Break Rule.** One segment is separated from the next by a 4px rule in the ink colour, the weight of the line. It is the heaviest horizontal rule in the system, and the first segment has none.

**The Tonal Depth Rule.** Surfaces separate by lightness, not by shadow. In light mode the paper is 95.5%, the quiet fill 91.5%, the code surface 98.5% and field white 99.5%. In dark mode the paper is 20%, the quiet fill 27.5%, fields 25% and the code surface 15.5%, with the sign band below all of them at 13%. Borders are opaque warm greys, not translucent white.

## Shapes

Two shape families. The diagram is drawn with circles and straight strokes: a 1.75rem round roundel, a 1.25rem ring with a 3px stroke, a 4px line and a 4px terminus bar. Controls are small rectangles with slightly eased corners: 0.375rem on buttons and inputs, 0.3rem on small buttons, 0.225rem on the stop row's hover fill and focus outline and on skeleton shapes. Borders are 1px. Nothing is pill-shaped except the roundel.

The review page adds to the first family and keeps the second. The section rail marks a segment with a square in the line colour, 13px with softened corners, so a segment reads apart from the rings of its sections. Finding marks are stops drawn on a 16-unit grid: a 7-unit disc or a 5.75-unit ring, at 0.875rem in a chip and 1rem in the gutter. Blast file marks are discs of 5px to 14px radius. A field mark is a 10px square with 2px corners and a 1.5px stroke. Diagram edges are 2px strokes that end in a small arrowhead; the imports of a Blast Diagram are 1.25px. Framed panels, boards, waypoint boxes and node plates take 0.3rem corners; popovers and the toast take 0.375rem. A changed node carries a 2px ink border in place of the 1px field stroke.

## Components

### Section Rail
The review page's navigation, in a 13rem column at the left. It reuses the 2.5rem rail column and the 4px line in the repository's colour. ### Line Roundel
A 1.75rem circle filled with the line colour, holding one bold letter in the line foreground. The letter comes from the backend. It is decorative for assistive technology; the repository name beside it is the heading.

### Stop Ring
The signature component. A 1.25rem ring centred on the line, in one state per branch status:
- **no-review**: hollow. Panel fill, 3px secondary-ink stroke.
- **awaiting-review**: half amber. Panel fill, amber stroke, the right half filled amber.
- **awaiting-reply**: amber ring with an amber centre dot.
- **stale**: broken. Panel fill, secondary-ink stroke dashed into short segments.
- **draft**: thin. Panel fill, 1.5px secondary-ink stroke.
- **closed**: solid secondary ink with a panel-coloured bar across it.
- **merged**: solid. Fill and stroke in the line colour.

A state change transitions fill, stroke, stroke width, dash pattern and the opacity of the inner marks at 180ms ease-out. The ring keeps its size and position in every state, so nothing reflows. The status filter reuses the same component at 1rem with a neutral line colour.

The `tone` prop is `status` by default, which draws each state as listed. `tone="signal"` draws the stroke in amber whatever the state. The review page's stale notice uses it on the broken ring, and that is its only use.

Lines of code on the code surface, in Code Block type. Each line is three columns: a right-aligned line number in the quiet syntax colour (3.25rem), a diff mark (1.25rem) and the code. A mark frames the exact columns a view names, such as a callback attribute or the name a value is declared under: a 22% tint of the line colour with a 1px outline in it and 0.225rem corners. Every use of a variable, function, class or type whose definition is known carries a dotted underline in secondary ink and takes the quiet fill on hover and focus, also where the definition is on the lines shown. Hovering or focusing it opens the definition in the frame of a Code Link's preview: the path in mono, the line range in Caption, the lines of the code block the name leads to as the change leaves them, set as a Code Block that does not wrap, then a note in secondary ink when the name comes from a library, and a link to the forge. Where a description is written for the definition, it sits between the header and the lines, closed by a hairline: Prose at Label size with 0.5rem between blocks. It is at most 12rem tall and scrolls by itself. The preview is as wide as its longest line of code, from 20rem to 64rem and never wider than the window less 2rem, and at most 30rem tall; a line longer than that scrolls sideways, and the prose inside wraps to the width the code sets. A name of the language has no lines: its preview is the name in mono, "Language" in Caption, the description and the note. The lines of a definition carry no definitions of their own.

The name an exported root code block is declared under carries the same underline, and its preview shows where the name is used: the name in mono and the count in Caption, such as "3 usages in 2 files", then each file that imports it, divided by hairlines: the path in mono at Caption size with a link to the forge, and the lines that use the name as a Code Block that does not wrap, each use framed by a mark. A name no file imports shows "No usages" in Caption and the note "No file imports it." in secondary ink. Only the first underlined name of a Code Block is in the tab order, and the left and right arrow keys move focus between the underlined names of the block. A screen reader reads the name itself, with the definition's path and line range, or the count of usages, as its description.

### Waypoint Mark
A closed waypoint in the Code Walkthrough and in a code view of a diagram's side panel: a button in the waypoint lane beside the first line the waypoint explains, the way an editor marks a breakpoint. It is a stop on the lane, drawn at 1rem: the waypoint's most urgent mark, in the order bug, risk, Core change, question, nit, as its Finding Mark; a waypoint with none is a ring with a 2.5 stroke in secondary ink; a waypoint with no write-up yet is the same ring dashed, turning while the agent writes it, only when motion is allowed; a reviewed waypoint is a solid disc in Waypoint Blue with a check cut from the code surface, at 45%. The current waypoint's plain ring turns Waypoint Blue with a centre dot, and any other current stop wears a 1.25 halo in Waypoint Blue. A waypoint in the reader's spotlight carries the four rays of the Spotlight Mark around whichever stop it shows. Two waypoints that start on the same line stack their marks. A mark is round, and takes the quiet fill on hover and while its waypoint is open. A closed mark carries the waypoint's anchor, so a jump or a code link lands on it.
- **Preview:** hovering or focusing the mark for 150ms opens a card framed like a Code Link's preview, 56rem wide and never wider than the window less 2rem. It sits on top of the marked line, 8px above it, with its left edge where the code text starts and a notch pointing down into the code. It opens under the same line, with the notch pointing up, only when there is no room above the line: under the sign band, or under the file header while the header is pinned. It slides sideways to stay in the window. It holds the Finding Marks with their labels, the title in Label Strong, the lines and the position as Label Segments in Caption secondary ink, then under a hairline the first paragraph of the reading cut at three lines, then under a hairline the card's footer: a small ghost "Expand" button with its R keycap that opens the box, and the Review Toggle at the right. The card closes when the page scrolls and never covers a pinned file header.
- **Marked lines:** while the card is open, the waypoint's lines are marked through Code Hike's mark annotation, from 4px before the code column to the end of the line: a 6% tint of the line colour with a 2px bar at 70% of it inside the left edge, fading in at 180ms ease-out. Code text sits 0.5rem inside that edge on every line, so the bar never touches it and nothing moves when a mark appears.
- **Stem:** a 2px line runs down the waypoint lane from the mark to the last line of every waypoint: a quiet rail in secondary ink at 35%, in Waypoint Blue at 50% while the waypoint is current, and solid Waypoint Blue while it is open, when it also joins the mark to the box above. Where waypoints nest, the rails join into one line down the declaration, and the strongest of them is drawn. A stem stops above and below every mark it passes instead of running through it.
- **Open:** clicking the mark or pressing Enter opens the Waypoint Box above the lines, and the page holds the clicked mark where it was. Expand waypoint (R) in the navigator opens the current waypoint's box and scrolls to it; Collapse waypoint (R) closes it and shows the mark's card again.
- **No write-up:** the card of a waypoint with no write-up shows, under its title, the waypoint's hint as one sentence in ink, then the Write-up Icon with a sentence in Label secondary ink that names the setup's agent: "Claude has not explained this line of code or checked it for issues yet.", or "This line of code is not explained or checked for issues yet." when no agent is set up. The footer starts with two small outline buttons before "Expand": "Let Claude explain" with its G keycap, led by the agent's mark at 0.875rem (Claude's in its own orange, OpenAI's for Codex and Cursor's in the button's ink), and "Explain the whole code block" with its Ctrl+G keycap, the second only when the waypoint sits in a root code block. The keycaps show on every card, and the keys act on the current waypoint. On the author's page the card says "This waypoint does not have any description or explanation." instead, and its footer offers a small outline "Write description" with its C keycap, after the two explain buttons, which opens the waypoint's form in place of its box. While the agent writes the waypoint, three skeleton bars take the place of the sentence, the last one shorter, pulsing only when motion is allowed, and the two buttons are gone; a screen reader hears "Claude is explaining this line." The page offers the buttons only when an agent is set up.
- **One card:** the page shows one card at a time. Hovering another mark, or stepping to another waypoint, closes the one that is open.
- **Stepping:** Previous waypoint and Next waypoint scroll the mark 14rem below the waypoint scroll margin, so the card has room above it, make the waypoint current and show its card once the scroll has settled, in place of opening the box.
- **Keyboard:** only the first gutter mark of a file is in the tab order, and the up and down arrow keys move between the marks of the file.

### Thread Mark
A closed comment thread: a button in the thread lane beside the last line the thread covers. A filled speech mark in secondary ink is an open thread, a thin dashed one is a resolved thread, and a hollow speech mark in amber text with an amber centre dot is a comment not sent yet; several threads on one line show the mark of the one that waits on the reviewer first. Hovering or focusing it opens the same card as a Waypoint Mark, at the same width: for each thread its state in bold (amber for a comment not sent yet) and its lines, then the first comment's Avatar at 1.5rem, the name in bold, the time and the body cut at two lines, and "Click or press Enter to open". Clicking it opens or closes the Comment Thread under its line. A comment not sent yet starts open, and every other thread starts closed.

### Waypoint Box
An open waypoint, set into the code at the lines it is about. A ruled box with 0.3rem corners on the panel colour, inside the code surface, indented so it starts where the code text starts (7rem in a Code Panel, 4.5rem where the code has no gutter); a nested waypoint steps in by a further 1.5rem per level. A waypoint with no lines on the page, such as one whose code is folded away, stays a box when closed. Its head is one row: a chevron, the Finding Mark with its label when the waypoint has a finding, the Core change mark when it has that note, the Spotlight Chip when it is in the spotlight, the title in Label Strong, the position ("3 of 12") in Caption and the Review Toggle. A waypoint whose text is not written yet shows its symbol name in mono secondary ink in place of a title.
- **Open:** the body follows a hairline: the reading, then the Core change note, then each finding, each divided by a hairline. A finding is its mark and title, its reading, a numbered trace and a small outline button that shows the proposed code in a framed Code Block.
- **Current:** the border turns to the line colour with a 1px outline in the same colour, so the box the navigator stepped to reads at a glance.
- **Reviewed:** the box drops to 60% opacity.
- Border, outline and opacity change at 180ms ease-out.

### Write-up Icon
A 0.875rem dashed ring, Lucide's circle-dashed, in secondary ink, that marks a part with no write-up. While the part is being written it spins, only when motion is allowed. Beside a band's heading it is followed by its label in Label secondary ink, 0.375rem apart.

### Finding Mark
A 0.875rem mark for the kind of a finding, usually followed by its label in bold Caption in the same tone.
- **bug**: a disc in error red with a diamond cut out of it, with its label in error red.
- **risk**: a ring with a 2.5 stroke in Risk Yellow and a small yellow diamond inside it, with its label in `risk-text`.
- **nit**: a thin ring with a 1.5 stroke and a short diagonal stroke inside it, in nit blue, with its label in nit blue.
- **question**: a ring with a 2 stroke and a question mark inside it, in ink, with its label in secondary ink.
- **core-change**: a disc in amber with a triangle cut out of it, with its label ("Core change") in amber text.

The cut-out shapes are holes, so each disc shows the surface it sits on.

### Spotlight Mark
A 0.875rem mark for code in the reader's spotlight: an ink ring with four short rays on its diagonals, like a lit lamp. In the gutter the rays alone sit around the waypoint's stop, so a finding in the spotlight shows both. Its shape is its own, so it never reads as a finding, and it stays out of amber, which belongs to the Core change mark beside it. The Spotlight Chip follows it with "Spotlight" in bold Caption ink, then each classification that put the code in the spotlight in Caption secondary ink, joined by "•". It sits after the Core change mark wherever both are drawn: in the head of a Waypoint Box, at the top of a waypoint's card, and on the first line of a root code block, before its Review Toggle, where a root block that holds a core change also carries the Core change mark.

### Review Toggle
The control that marks a waypoint or a file as reviewed. A 1.75rem ghost control in Control Small: a 1rem ring with a 1.5px secondary-ink stroke, then the label. When pressed the ring fills with the line colour and shows a check in the line foreground. Colour and the check change at 180ms ease-out.

### Review Navigator
The keyboard-first toolbar of the review page, always on screen: a second sign band fixed to the foot of the window, 2.75rem tall in `sign` ink, running the full width of the page up to the chat panel when it is open and across the whole window in full screen, with the 4px rule in the line colour along its upper edge, where it meets the page, as the band at the top has along its lower edge. Its controls sit on it centred on the main column, between the section rail and the chat panel when it is open. In full screen they centre on the window. It never leaves the window or slides under the chat panel: when centring would push it past an edge it moves in to 1.5rem from that edge, over the section rail if it must. When it is still wider than that space it gets denser until it fits, first with Back and Next without the section they name, then with only the keycaps, each control keeping its label as a tooltip and for screen readers. The band takes no shadow.

It opens with "Jump to" and its key, then Back and Next, then the controls of the section most present on screen, in groups divided by 1px rules at 20% of `sign-foreground`. When that section changes its controls fade in over 180ms and the navigator centres again on its new width. Each control is a band button 1.875rem tall in Control Small: its label, then a keycap in mono bold Micro, with a 1px border and a 2px lower border at 35% of its ink. A switch that is on inverts to `sign-foreground` with bold ink text. "Mark as reviewed" is the one Primary Amber button; once the waypoint is reviewed it reads "Reviewed" and inverts like a switch. Filter inverts while its menu is open, and Jump to takes a 14% fill. A key that is pressed flashes its button at 25% for 180ms. A control that cannot act is at 45%.

| Section | Controls |
|---|---|
| Every section | Jump to (W), Back: the section before (A), Next: the section after (D) |
| Context | Expand all or Collapse all (Q), when the context has accordion sections |
| Trace Diagram | Collapse all or Expand all (Q), then on the author's page Add card (C), Clear diagram (K) and Remove diagram (L), then Full screen (F) |
| Blast Diagram | Export bound (E) and Import bound (Q) when it has both views, Full screen (F) |
| Code Walkthrough | Previous waypoint (A), the position in the segment ("4 of 8") once a waypoint is current, Next waypoint (D), Mark as reviewed (S), Add comment (C), or Write description (C) on the author's page, Filter (F), Collapse all or Expand all (Q), Collapse file or Expand file (E), Expand waypoint or Collapse waypoint (R) |

Jump to and Filter open upward as menus with the popover fill and a 1px ink border, and a footer in Micro secondary ink that names their keys as small keycaps. Jump to lists the sections of the segment on screen, each with a ring that is filled for the one on screen and marked "Here", then a hairline, "Other segments" and each other segment with its square, whose sections open to the right, then the sections outside every segment such as the Conversation.

Back and Next step through the sections in the order of the rail, from one segment into the next. Each names the section it goes to ("Back: Context", "Next: Trace Diagram"), or the segment and its position ("Next: Segment (2/3)") when it goes into another segment. The first section has no Back and the last no Next. The Code Walkthrough keeps Previous waypoint and Next waypoint, and they step through the waypoints of its own segment only. From its first waypoint Previous waypoint becomes Back to the section before ("Back: Blast Diagram"), and from its last waypoint Next waypoint becomes Next to the next segment.

Going to a Code Walkthrough with Back, Next or Jump to makes the first waypoint it shows current, without scrolling past the heading, so Mark as reviewed is ready. A control that cannot act and knows why, such as Mark as reviewed, Add comment or Expand waypoint with no waypoint current, stays at 45% but answers a click, its key or hovering with a tooltip that says what to do: "Pick a waypoint first: press D to step to the next one." Add comment on a waypoint with no lines on the page says "This waypoint has no lines on the page to comment on."

With no waypoint of the segment current, Next waypoint starts at the first one below the reading line and Previous waypoint at the last one above it. With none above, Previous waypoint is Back; with none below, Next waypoint is Next. Stepping to a waypoint makes it current, shows its Waypoint Mark's card and dims every code row of its file outside the waypoint's lines to 45% at 180ms ease-out; on the author's page, where waypoints are boxes, it opens the box instead. Scrolling more than 160px away, or until less than a third of it is in view, ends the dimming; the waypoint stays current. Marking a waypoint reviewed while reviewed waypoints are hidden steps on to the next. A Code Walkthrough the reader has scrolled into keeps its place when it changes size: Collapse all or Expand all, a filter tick and the Reviewed tick bring its heading back under the band, the filter ones once the filtered walkthrough has arrived, and collapsing one file keeps that file's header where it was. A key does nothing while a field or a dialog has the keyboard, or with a modifier held. In full screen the navigator shows inside the board.

### Comment Thread
Reviewers' comments on a line, set into the code under that line on field white with a field stroke and 0.3rem corners, indented to the code text and held to 52rem. It shows while its Thread Mark is open. Each comment is an Avatar at 1.5rem, the name in bold, the time in Caption and the body as Prose. A comment that is not yet submitted carries its pending label in bold amber Caption. A pending comment also carries "Discard", a quiet underlined Caption button. A thread on a range or on a removed line opens with a Caption row in secondary ink that names its lines. A quiet field at the end opens the composer, and beside it a small outline button resolves or reopens the thread, where the code host takes it. The composer is the Text Editor at Label size with a 5rem minimum height, then a row with a hint in Caption at the left (the composer on a line says how to stretch it to a range), a small ghost "Cancel" and a small primary submit. The composer on a line has a small outline "Add single comment" between the two, which sends the comment at once where the primary submit adds it to the review. A resolved thread is folded to its Caption row: the lines, "Resolved" in bold ink, and a quiet underlined "Show" that unfolds it, then "Hide". The Conversation band uses the same entries and composer without the box, divided by hairlines; an entry of a thread the page cannot place carries its file and lines in mono Caption under its name.

### Diagram Board
The framed surface a diagram is drawn on, with 0.3rem corners and a 16px dot grid in the hairline colour. On the page it is as tall as its diagram at the current zoom, at most 70% of the window's height; a taller diagram is panned inside it. Its view controls are three Icon Buttons in a control group at its top right, Zoom in, Zoom out and Full screen, which reads Leave full screen and turns its arrows inward in full screen; the zoom buttons step it by 1.25. Full screen is also the last control of the Review Navigator for the diagram, with its key F, and is drawn pressed in full screen. Opening full screen puts the focus on the full-screen view itself, so no button's tooltip shows; Tab then reaches the view controls. Scrolling the mouse wheel over the board, on the page or in full screen, zooms it smoothly instead of scrolling the page: up zooms in, down zooms out, and the point under the pointer stays where it is. The zoom runs from 0.41 to 1.95 of the board's own size. The group sits 0.5rem in from the top and the right edge of the board; while the board is taller than the window it stays 0.5rem below the rows pinned under the band, and in full screen it is fixed 0.5rem below the top of the board, left of the side panel. Its buttons are 1.75rem ghost squares with their name as the tooltip. On the author's page an action of the board, such as removing a selected line, goes before the zoom buttons, divided from them by a 1rem hairline. In full screen the board fills the window, and selecting a node opens a side panel at the right, up to 41.8rem wide and divided from the board by a hairline. The hairline is the panel's resize handle: it turns to ink on hover and while it is dragged, dragging it sets the panel's width between 20rem and four fifths of the window, the left and right arrow keys move it by 2rem when it has focus, and a double click gives the panel its own width back. The chosen width is kept in the browser and is the width of every diagram's panel, on this visit and the next. The panel holds the node's title, its written description and its code views. A code view is headed by what it shows in bold Caption secondary ink (Declaration, Data, Event or Render) with its count after a bullet, then the path in mono and the line range in Caption, then the lines between two hairlines, and under them a note in secondary ink for a file the change did not touch and a link to the forge. The lines are drawn exactly as in a Code Panel, with the same gutter: Code Blocks, here wrapped, a row on the quiet fill for hidden lines, and a Waypoint Mark beside the first line each waypoint explains, with its card and its stem, which opens the Waypoint Box above the lines. A code view has no comment control, and its thread lane stays empty. A component shows Data, Event and Render in that order, a handler shows Event, and any other node shows its Declaration. Positions and sizes of everything on the board come from the backend.

### Diagram Nodes and Edges
A node is a flat ruled plate: field white, a field stroke, 0.3rem corners. Its head opens with a header bar, closed by a hairline on a faint tint of ink: the icon of its type, the name of its code in bold mono, and at the right its path in mono Micro, cut to its last two folders and its file. Under the bar are the Micro line of its type, followed by its classification after a "•" when the two differ, and the Node Label, so the path never takes room from what is written. A node inside another is drawn inside its plate, on the muted field at every other depth, and a trigger label is a ruled row inside its node with its icon, its label on one line and the name of its code in mono Micro on the line below, so the name never takes width from the label. A trigger label that is not written yet is a row of one line. The parameters of a function, a method, a hook or a component are no rows: they sit under the head as chips in mono Micro secondary ink on the muted field, 20px tall with a hairline border and 0.225rem corners, 4px apart and wrapping onto further lines, each its name and its type as written, `session: Session`, and a chip wider than the node is cut with an ellipsis and shows itself whole as a tooltip. A listed block of a node (a state, a hook, an effect, a call or a step) is a row of the same kind, with its label over the name of its code once it is written, and the name of its code alone in mono secondary ink until then. In a component the rows are grouped, each group under a Micro heading in bold uppercase secondary ink that names it, with Triggers above Steps. In any other node the rows have no heading and follow the order the code is written in, its arguments first. A node the change touches carries a 2px ink border. A node whose text is not written yet shows its symbol name in mono secondary ink. Hovering or selecting a node draws a 2px outline in the line colour at 180ms ease-out.

Edges are 2px strokes in secondary ink. Every edge of the Trace Diagram is one solid line with a filled arrowhead that carries no label. Between two columns that follow each other it is straight, from the right edge of one node towards the left edge of the next, and it bends 24px before the node so that the arrowhead arrives level. The edges that reach the same head or row arrive 18px apart, closer where the row has no room for that. An edge that passes a column crosses it level, in a lane between the nodes of that column, and the edges that pass a column to the same node run side by side, 18px apart, never as one line. A node is set level with the row or the head its edge leaves. Nodes of one column that would overlap there are set 32px apart as one run, centred on the heights they each reach for, so a crowded column spreads above and below its edges and the board does not grow only downward. The whole board then moves so its highest node sits at the top margin. Anyone reading a review that is stored on their machine, its author or a reviewer, can drag a top node to another place; the drop saves the place of every top node into the review artifact, so the board opens the same way next time. A fixture and a published review keep their nodes where the layout puts them. An edge that closes a loop turns at right angles: it leaves the right edge of its node, runs 16px below the nodes it passes, the next such edge 18px further out, and reaches the left edge of the node it leads back to.

A diagram is headed by its description alone, as the Context band is by its context: the caption of the diagram, as prose held to 55rem, 1rem above the board. A description not written yet is a skeleton of three bars while an agent is writing or awaited, and "Not written yet." in secondary ink otherwise. Nothing else is set above the board: no title of the diagram, no count of nodes and no instructions. On the author's page the band's heading is followed by the link button "Edit" where the segment has a diagram, and pressing it puts the text editor of the Context band in place of the description, with "Cancel" and "Save" below it.

A review without segments shows its Trace Diagrams as tabs under the band's heading, drawn as the Context Tabs are: the diagrams' names in bold Label secondary ink, 1.5rem apart, over a hairline, the open one in ink with a 2px underline in the line colour, 1rem above the open diagram's description. The first tab is open; Left and Right move between them, Home and End go to the first and the last. The author edits the open tab's diagram as a segment's. A review without segments has no Segment Title, and on the author's page no "Add a segment" and no segment editing.

On the author's page the Trace Diagram is edited in place. The band's heading is followed by the link button "Add a diagram" where the segment has none. The Review Navigator holds the diagram's own actions in a group after Collapse all: Add card (C), Clear diagram (K), disabled while the diagram has no card, when its hint says "This diagram has no card yet.", and Remove diagram (L). Clear diagram and Remove diagram ask first, from the key and from the button alike: a dialog titled "Clear this diagram?" or "Remove this diagram?" says what goes, with a ghost Cancel, which holds the focus, and a destructive button named after the action. Escape or Cancel keeps the diagram. A line being drawn is a 2px curve in signal amber from the dot to the pointer, and it may be dropped anywhere on a card: it ends at the side nearest to the drop, the left, the top or the bottom. Dropped on the empty board it stays, and ends at a plus: a 32px round button with a dashed border in signal amber on the card fill, titled "Create Card", set with the middle of its left edge where the line was dropped. Pressing the plus opens the Add Card dialog, whose description says the line ends at the new card, and the card is set with the middle of its head's left edge where the plus was. The plus and its line are not saved: they go when the card is created, when another line is dropped on the board, and when the empty board is pressed. Every row carries a 10px dot at the right edge of its node, where a line starts, and every node a dot at the middle of its left, its top and its bottom edge, where a line ends: a ring in signal amber on the card fill, at 60% until hovered. A node's menu gains a pencil and a bin before its two buttons, also on a node inside another. A pressed line is drawn as selected, and a bin, "Remove the selected line", joins the board's view controls before the zoom buttons while one is. A node that was dragged stays where it was dropped, and a line to or from it runs straight from the row or the head it leaves to 24px before the node it reaches; a line that ends at the top or the bottom of a node arrives at the middle of that edge, straight down or straight up. The Add Card and the Modify Card dialogs fill the window but 2rem on each side, as the Code Link dialog does: Add Card sets the list of the file's code blocks, 16rem wide, at the left of the code, and Modify Card sets the card's label, its description and its rows in a 26rem column at the left of the code. A row of that list is a bordered box with the select of its kind, its lines in secondary ink and a bin on one line, and its label below.

### Missing write-up on a diagram
A Trace Diagram card with no label shows Flight Plan's hint where the label goes, in Micro secondary ink at regular weight, two lines at most, so the hint never reads as the agent's bold label. A row with no label keeps its code name and shows its hint as its tooltip. A description that is not written shows the diagram's hint in secondary ink. In full screen the side panel of such a card opens with the hint in ink, then the Write-up Icon with "Not explained yet." in Label secondary ink, then a small outline "Explain this card" button with its G keycap; the Review Navigator holds Explain this card (G) after Collapse all while the panel is open. While the agent writes the card, the same three skeleton bars as the waypoint card take the place of the line and the button. The Blast Diagram's side panel offers the same for a file nobody assessed: what the panel says of the file, then "Not assessed yet." and "Assess this file" (G), or the three skeleton bars while the agent assesses it; a file of the change offers nothing.

### Blast Diagram
A graph of files on a Diagram Board, with no plates or zones. The files stand in columns read from left to right: a file is at the left of every file it imports, as far right as its longest chain of imports puts it, and in its column beside the files it is joined to. The board opens centred. A file is a Blast File Mark with its label under it in mono Micro on the panel colour, and under that, for a file of the change, a file that breaks or a file whose behaviour changes, what the mark says in Micro secondary ink. A file that is unaffected, not assessed or not reached is drawn plain, with nothing under its label, and its label is secondary ink; every other file's is foreground. A file is drawn larger the more files it is connected to. An import is a straight 1.25px stroke in secondary ink at 55% opacity, from the rim of the file that imports to the rim of the file it imports, ending in a small filled arrowhead.

Hovering, focusing or selecting a file keeps it and the files joined to it: its label turns bold, the labels of the joined files and the imports between them turn to foreground at full strength, and every other file drops to 25% opacity and every other import to 15%, at 180ms ease-out. Selecting a file opens the board's side panel with the path as its title in bold mono and where the file stands above the title (Added, Breaks, Unaffected, Not assessed and so on), then its impact reason as Prose, or one fixed sentence in secondary ink for a plain file, the files that import it and the files it imports as rows in mono that select that file, and a small outline button to its File Section when it has one. On the author's page a file the change reaches also has an "Edit impact" link button under what the panel says about it. It replaces the impact reason or the fixed sentence with a form: a Select Field for the impact type, the Text Editor for the reason when the type takes one, and Cancel and Save at the right.

The Review Navigator starts the Blast Diagram's controls with one button per view, "Export bound" (E) and "Import bound" (Q), each titled with what the view traces. The button of the view that shows is a switch that is on. A diagram with one view has no such buttons. The counts and the key above the board are those of the view that shows.

Above the board two rows in Label: the counts as Label Segments, then the key in secondary ink, with each Blast File Mark the diagram draws beside its label and an arrow beside what it points at.

### Blast File Mark
The mark of a file on a Blast Diagram, always with a label. A file of the change is a disc inside a 1.5px ring, 3px out, in the colour of what the diff did to it. A file the change did not touch has no ring.
- **added**: green (`diff-added`), with a plus in the panel colour.
- **modified**: blue (`line-blue`), with nothing on the disc.
- **deleted**: red (`diff-removed`), with a bar in the panel colour.
- **breaks**: a disc in error red with a cross in the panel colour.
- **behaviour-changes**: an ink ring with its right half filled.
- **plain**: a plain disc in secondary ink, for a file that is unaffected, not assessed or not reached by the change.

The three colours of a changed file are the only place a Blast Diagram uses the blue of the line palette as a fixed colour: there it means modified, whatever the repository's line colour is.

### Chat Panel
A panel docked at the right edge under the sign band, 26rem wide, on the panel colour with a hairline at its left. Its head carries the heading in Subtitle, the agent's state and a close control. The state is a small ring that differs by shape and colour: hollow in secondary ink when the agent is not running, broken in the line colour while it starts, solid in the line colour while it works, hollow in the line colour while a session the reviewer started listens, solid amber while it waits for the reviewer's answers, and hollow in the destructive colour when it could not start, with the reason in destructive Caption under the head. Under the head sits the model select of the agent, with a Caption hint that the agent uses it from its next turn. Messages are a bold author label, a time in Caption and the body as Prose; a code reference sits above the body in mono Micro on the quiet fill. The composer is a text area and a small primary send button.

A question batch is a bordered card among the messages: its heading in bold, its time, and its status in Caption, bold amber text while it waits for an answer, with an amber border. An open batch is a form: each question is a fieldset with the prompt as its bold legend, the detail of a permission request in mono Micro on the quiet fill, the options as radios, or checkboxes when several picks are allowed, each with its description in Caption, and a text area for the reviewer's own words; one small primary button sends the batch. A settled batch shows each prompt with its answer. The panel slides in over 200ms ease-out while the page gives up its width.

Selecting code on the page opens a small toolbar above the selection, with a 1px ink border, that adds a comment on the lines or sends a reference to them to the chat.

### Toast
A one-line confirmation or error at the bottom centre of the window: popover fill, 1px ink border, 0.375rem corners, Label text, no shadow. It fades in over 150ms.

### Guide Tooltip
A tip for a reader who is new to the review page, pointing at the control or the part it is about. It is a small sign in `sign` ink, 22rem wide and never wider than the window less 2rem, with 0.375rem corners, the 4px rule in the line colour along its top edge and an ink arrow towards its anchor. It takes no shadow; in dark mode, where the ink sits below the panel, a hairline at 20% of `sign-foreground` keeps its edge. It holds a title in Subtitle and the tip in Label at 88% of `sign-foreground`, where a key the tip names is drawn as a navigator keycap. At its bottom right is one Primary Amber button, "Okay, got it!" with its Enter keycap, and at its top right a close control in `sign-muted`. Pressing the close control turns the same sign into a question, "Keep showing tips?", with a quiet "Stop tips" and a primary "Keep tips"; "Stop tips" turns every tip off. The page is not dimmed and the tip never takes focus. Enter and Escape act on the newest tip only while no field, menu or dialog has the keyboard: Enter is "Okay, got it!", Escape asks the question. A tip fades in over 180ms, still when motion is reduced, and Base UI's positioner flips and shifts it to stay in the window. It hides while its anchor is off screen.

Tips are not a tour. Each shows on its own, five seconds after its conditions hold, and several can show at once. The conditions are the section most present on screen with the views on screen inside it (the Change Overview, an open waypoint card or box), features the reader has used or not, earlier tips, days since the first visit, whether the review is the reader's own, and values the page passes. Every button of the review page carries `data-user-action-id` and every section `data-view-section-id`; one click and hover listener logs every action, whether a tip asks for it or not, and a navigator key logs the same action as its button. The record lives in browser storage under `flightplan-guide-tooltips`, one per origin: the app, each published Flight Plan and the site demo keep their own.

The review page shows eight tips, all on a teammate's review: Jump to (W) in the Context section, the Change Overview cards from the second day, a changed Trace Diagram card, a changed Blast Diagram file once a Trace Diagram card was opened, the waypoint marks in the Code Walkthrough, "Let Claude explain" (G) on a waypoint with no explanation, Next waypoint (D) once a mark was hovered, and Filter (F) after five steps between waypoints.

### Buttons
Compact and quiet. One primary action per view.
- **Shape:** eased rectangle (0.375rem), 2rem tall, 0.625rem horizontal padding, Label size.
- **Primary:** Primary Amber fill with ink text in bold; hover mixes 12% ink into the fill. Used for "Add repository", "Add", "Choose" and "Try again".
- **Outline, small:** field-white fill with a field stroke, 1.75rem tall, 0.8rem text; hover in quiet fill. Used for "Create Flight Plan" on each line and for the secondary controls of the review page. A small outline button that is a switch inverts to ink with bold text while it is on.
- **Ghost:** no fill at rest, quiet fill on hover. Used for "Cancel".
- **Focus:** focus-blue border with a 3px ring at half strength. **Disabled:** 50% opacity, no pointer events.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** draw status with a Stop Ring and a text label together.
- **Do** set `--line` through `LINE_COLOR_CLASSES` from the key the backend sends, and read `--line` everywhere a line colour is drawn.
- **Do** keep the 2.5rem rail column and the 4px line weight on every surface that shows a line, so all lines register.
- **Do** separate sections with a 1px hairline and space, open each repository's line with the 2px ink rule, and separate one segment from the next with the 4px ink rule.
- **Do** keep every neutral warm, and separate surfaces by the lightness steps of the Tonal Depth Rule.
- **Do** frame a file's code panel and a diagram board with a 1px hairline border, and rule what is set into the code with the field stroke.
- **Do** give every new mark its own shape, and a text label beside it.
- **Do** draw a diagram edge solid for a call, dashed for a value and dotted for data, with a filled arrowhead when the connection is proven and a hollow one when it is assumed.
- **Do** put a page's header in the sign band's slot, and ask for the full width when the page needs it.
- **Do** set branch names, forge slugs, paths, symbol names and code in Overpass Mono, and everything else in Overpass.
- **Do** join label segments with "•" (U+2022).
- **Do** centre single-line text in controls, avatars and roundels by its capital height with the `text-box-cap` utility; Overpass sits high in its line box otherwise.
- **Do** transition state in place at 180ms ease-out, and hold decorative pulsing behind the reduced-motion preference.
- **Do** render the labels, counts, colours and order the backend sends.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use amber outside the logo, the primary button and what waits on the reviewer: the awaiting-review and awaiting-reply statuses (on the index page and in the review header), and on the review page the drafting strip, the stale notice, an out-of-date artifact's label, the Core change mark and a pending comment's label.
- **Don't** wrap content in cards, or add shadows. Code panels, diagram boards and the plates of the Change Overview are the only framed content. Menus, popovers and dialogs are the overlays, and they are set off by a 1px ink border.
- **Don't** use a third weight, or a third typeface.
- **Don't** use "·" (U+00B7) as a separator; Overpass draws it with no space after it.
- **Don't** show status as a coloured badge or pill; the ring is the status mark.
- **Don't** tell two marks, two edges or two arrowheads apart by colour alone.
- **Don't** change the size or position of a ring or a row when its state changes.
- **Don't** choose a line colour, compute a count or build a label in a component.
